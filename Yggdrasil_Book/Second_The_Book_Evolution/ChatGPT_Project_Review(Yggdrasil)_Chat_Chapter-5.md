1) The Adversary’s Advantage
----------------------------

Smart opponents don’t attack strength; they attack timing, measurement, and monoculture. They ride latency (you act late), game metrics (your dials lie), and exploit single stacks (one fault, global outage). 2) The Defect Inequality
------------------------

Use one rule to size deterrence and response:

**G×R ≤ 0.3×p×S×L**

*   **G** (attacker gain): financial/power upside per cycle.
*   **R** (repeatability): how easily the attack can be run again.
*   **p** (detection probability): chance you catch it per cycle.
*   **S** (sanction): certain, bond-backed loss on detection.
*   **L** (latency inverse): how fast detection→sanction runs (shorter delay, higher L).

Push **p, S, L** up; pull **G, R** down. If you cannot make the inequality hold, you don’t have governance—you have wishful thinking.

Chapter 5 — Adversarial Stress Test: How to Beat Smart Opponents Without Becoming Them
======================================================================================

The Adversary’s Advantage
-------------------------

Smart opponents don’t attack your strongest points; they work your blind spots—timing, measurement, and monoculture. They ride latency so your decision arrives after the money moved. They game the instruments you watch so the dashboards look “all green” while real users bleed. And they bet on single stacks so one bad patch becomes a region-wide outage. Continuity Modulation (CM) answers with the Rails and Pillars—exit that stays real, neutral recourse that moves quickly, and multi-implementation standards that refuse correlated failure (see Ch. 2 Rails; Pillars recap in Ch. 1). This chapter turns principle into operating practice. We will price sanctions to domains, compress decision latency with fast tribunals, keep corridors anti-cartel, inoculate standards against capture, harden “rescue first” against abuse, and design fallbacks that switch off on schedule. The aim is steady hands and bounded authority: **sanctions-before-shots** in daily use; the **CUCC cage** only under **AHF**—narrow, clocked, and audited—when the Rails themselves need reopening (see Ch. 6 AHF).

The Defect Inequality
---------------------

Use one rule to size deterrence and response:

**G × R ≤ 0.3 × p × S × L**

Where **G** is attacker gain per cycle, **R** is repeatability, **p** is detection probability, **S** is sanction on detection, and **L** is the inverse of delay from detection to sanction (shorter delay, larger L). The inequality converts governance from posture to math. Your job is to raise **p, S, L** while cutting **G, R**—and to keep doing it as tactics shift. If you cannot make the inequality hold in a domain, you do not have governance in that domain; you have wishful thinking.

Two practical notes. First, never price **S** from a headline incident; price it from **domain profiles** where gains and repeats actually accumulate (traffic slots, cooling credits, replication rights, registry control). Second, treat **L** as a system property you can engineer: pre-wired escrows, prepared order templates, and standing panels cut days into hours. The inequality is not a slogan. It is your budget, your architecture, and your audit plan in one line.

The Threat Map You Must Assume
------------------------------

Treat the following as ambient risk, not edge cases: bond arbitrage across underpriced domains; corridor cartels that squeeze exit; standards capture via registry committees; SAR abuse where staged distress drives policy; legal DDoS that clogs tribunals; metric gaming that flatters KPIs while service craters; fallback trigger manufacture to force emergency powers; replication gray zones that launder lineages; cooling credit corners; compute dark pools; refugee weaponization aimed at breaking corridors; memetic or ombuds capture that fixes narratives rather than problems; vendor–state collusion via exclusive interfaces; latency arbitrage through slow jurisdictions; and ethical booby-traps designed to maneuver you into the CUCC cage. You will meet composites of these, not singletons. Plan accordingly.

Pricing Sanctions and Beating Latency
-------------------------------------

Deterrence is arithmetic. Tie sanctions to **rights**—ports, slots, registry seats—rather than to abstract “entities.” Price a domain’s sanction capacity with a conservative formula:

**S\_d = k · (Ĝ · R̂) / (p̂ · L̂)** with **k ≥ 3**.

Here **Ĝ, R̂, p̂, L̂** are conservative estimates (last-quarter data plus stress multipliers) and **k** is a policy guard against optimism. Publish bond schedules per domain. Make ledgers visible to counterparties. Offer rebates for clean operation so the efficient frontier rewards the operators you want to grow.

Latency is the other half. Wire **Interim Orders (IO) ≤ 72h** with a **reversible tranche** capped at **≤ 20% of S**. Wire **Final Orders (FO) ≤ 14–30 days** with public reasons. Pre-authorize standing panels. Pre-draft order templates by scenario. Pre-fund escrows and bond wallets so the button press actually moves money on IO, then trues up on FO. This is how **L** becomes a lever—not a plea.

The discipline remains **sanctions-before-shots**. You burn ports, claw back slots, deny schedules, and adjust fees to reopen the Rail. Force is reserved to unjam the Rail where sanctions cannot reach in time; it is not how you settle scores.

Anti-Cartel Corridors
---------------------

Exit is the oxygen of plural life. If corridors are captured, everything else is theater. Publish corridor SLAs that keep capacity ahead of pressure and make manipulation expensive. Concretely: maintain elasticity at or above **95th-percentile demand + X%**; run **commit–reveal maintenance** with public notices **T–14 days** before major work; cap the **price slope** (e.g., **≤ +10% per 7 days** absent declared emergency). Instrument for cartel signals: HHI above 2,200 for 30 days, price slope that detaches from demand, slot churn patterns inconsistent with maintenance windows, and green-lane uptime that sags without exogenous load.

When dual signals fire, use **joint red cards**: place the chokepoint under a 90-day trustee, claw back 10% of slots for community carriers, burn the corridor’s domain bonds at **0.3·S\_d**, and publish the recovery schedule. The action is visible, bounded, and reversible. Note how this expresses the **Pillars** without re-teaching them: reliability drills and reserve funds live under Skill/Reliability; corridor reserves under Land/Habitat; green-lane quotas protect Birth/Crew and Language/Codec; and Security stays bounded by the Rails.

Standards Capture Countermeasures
---------------------------------

Do not permit a singleton to sit on a public rail. Require **≥ 3 certified implementations per rail** backed by open conformance suites and publicly versioned test vectors. Enforce a **45/30/25 anti-concentration rule** across market share, reference deployments, and registry control. Rotate editors on fixed terms with disclosed conflicts and quorum engines for merges. Place **bug bounties in escrow**, payable across stacks without vendor permission to file. When a patch goes wrong, roll back under AHF scope to **reopen the Rail**, then publish the postmortem and leave a **12-month grace window** for migration so cost does not invite quiet recapture (see Ch. 7 Standards).

SAR Abuse and Ethical Traps
---------------------------

“Rescue first” is a Rail. Treat it like one. Authenticate distress with hardware beacons plus two-factor and location proofs. Route calls through **neutral triage hubs** that publish same-day logs (names redacted) so narrative capture does not reroute boats. Rotate crews to prevent local capture and burnout. Enforce a **triple bond burn** on confirmed false distress with a **180-day SAR-mute** for the abusing actor. Keep green lanes live for essentials and keep force out of the picture unless the Rail is physically blocked. This is how you protect a non-negotiable value without letting it become the attacker’s favorite exploit.

Legal DDoS and Metric Goodhart
------------------------------

Flooded dockets are an old trick; dashboards without uncertainty are a new one. For the first, require **filing stakes** for high-volume litigants, refundable when ≥ 20% of claims survive IO/FO. Triage queues by Rail impact with neutral heuristics that you publish weekly. Expand panels only when throughput, not politics, demands it.

For the second, pair outcome metrics with process audits, and publish **uncertainty bands** and **sensitivity notes** on every dial. Run randomized audits at 5–10% and adversarial re-labeling on samples. Watch for **metric drift** with simple statistics (e.g., CUSUM > 3σ) and—when it fires—retest across stacks. Never worship the dial; show your work, and make it costly to lie to you with your own instruments.

Fallback Triggers That Don’t Stick
----------------------------------

Define **2-of-3 independent triggers** (for example, outage correlation **ρ ≥ 0.6** across **≥ 2** stacks; corridor uptime **< 97%** on two green lanes; verified SAR surge **\> 3σ**), then require a **two-key scope box**—a civil key and an independent technical key—and keep the mission narrow: **reopen the Rails** only. Put **hard sunsets at 7/30/90 days**. If someone pushes to escalate, require a **petitioner bond** that burns when claims fail post-hoc. Preserve a **Golden Chain of Custody**—**logs before labels**—so post-crisis truth can’t be rewritten. And when the CUCC cage closes, publish the re-federalization timetable with dates, not vibes (see Ch. 6 AHF).

Thermo-Finance and Cooling Markets
----------------------------------

Cooling is a cross-rail dependency that can be cornered like any commodity. Design markets the way you design safety systems. Use AMMs with circuit breakers and TWAP oracles. Cap **position limits** so any single actor holds **≤ 15%** of open interest; auto-throttle to **0.5× baseline** on breach pending investigation. Hold **14–21 days** of average demand in strategic reserves with rules for release that are pre-published. Scan for **dark-pool spreads** by comparing ledgered trades to measured draw; alert on deviations **\> 8%** for **48h**. Keep price slope caps congruent with corridor caps to avoid cross-domain gaming. Declare emergency only through AHF triggers, not press releases.

Replication Gray Zones and Lineage
----------------------------------

Lineage controls are how you respect creativity while protecting the commons, across human, biological, cybernetic, and synthetic forms. Require **signed component trees** and rate-limited releases. Run **teardown lotteries** that destructively validate claimed configs. Allow **de-minimis caps** for small unlicensed replication with consumable keys, but escalate when aggregation trips published thresholds. Use **“honey buyer” tests**—controlled offers designed to detect illicit resale or lineage laundering. Keep **two-key overrides** ready for public rails; always audit their use after the fact. This is **leashed replication** in practice: provenance as a safety belt, not a choke chain.
