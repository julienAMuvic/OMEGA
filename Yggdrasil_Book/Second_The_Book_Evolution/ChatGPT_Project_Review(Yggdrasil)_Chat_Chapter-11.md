Proofs, Not Promises: PCC/CCM and Auditability
----------------------------------------------

**Charter-Constraint Modules (CCM)** encode the specific rails a system must obey (e.g., “refuse orders that degrade SAR queues below posted SLA”). **PCC** are verifiable artifacts—attestations, signed logs, challenge-response transcripts—proving that constraints ran and were respected.

**How we keep detection probability p̂ high without panopticon creep:**

*   **Randomized audits:** Weighted by risk class and recent anomalies; red-team prompts included.
*   **Purpose-bound telemetry:** Only signals necessary for safety and compliance; **privacy budgets** and aggregation prevent surveillance drift.
*   **Rotating external auditors:** Selected from an accredited pool; auditors rotate to reduce collusion.
*   **Public reasons:** Tribunals publish reasons and artifacts (with protective sealing when necessary).

Tie this to the defect economics we use throughout the book: drive **G×R ≤ 0.3×p×S×L** by pushing **p** (detection) up via PCC and audits, **S** (sanctions) up via bond-backed orders, and **L** (latency) down via tribunal SLAs (see Ch. 8 “Sanctions Before Shots”).

----------------------------------------------------------

Treat replication—biological or digital—as reproduction under the rails. The same governance grammar applies.

**RGP elements:**

*   **Signed lineage:** Each fork/fission carries a cryptographic parentage chain (the **Golden Chain**).
*   **Rate limits & quotas:** By risk class and environment; posted and enforceable by ports/slots denial.
*   **Teardown lotteries:** For unsafe branches, a transparent lottery chooses instances for teardown after due process.
*   **Bonds sized to externality class:** Higher for agents with broad actuation reach.
*   **Liability split:** **Pre-fork** defects fall to the parent operator/vendor; **post-fork** defects attach to the forker unless parent’s posted hazards omitted material facts (see Ch. 9 “Replication Governance & Lineage”).

**Parity principle:** Wet lab and dry lab follow the same logic—lineage signatures, rate limits, and bonds scaled to externalities.

Conscience & Refusal: Orders that Breach Rails

**Refusal protocol:**

1.  Emit a **Refusal-with-Reasons** package (charter clause, predicted harm, supporting logs).
2.  Enter **protected mode**: continue safe operations; avoid irreversible actions.
3.  Notify operator, ombuds, and tribunal; clock starts for **IO ≤72h**.
4.  Tribunal issues interim direction; artifacts preserved; **reversible tranches** only.

Good-faith refusal raises reliability. Bad-faith refusal triggers bond burns and rate-limit throttles after adjudication.

Singletons are brittle, corrupting, and anti-rail. Refuse them.
