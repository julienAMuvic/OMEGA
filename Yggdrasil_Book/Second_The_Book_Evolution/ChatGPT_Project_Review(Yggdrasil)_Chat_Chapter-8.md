1) The Case for Sanctions-First
-------------------------------

Force is blunt, slow, and expensive. It shatters legitimacy and corrodes the very rails we need to survive. Predictable, bond-backed sanctions—delivered through the public rails of ports, slots, and corridors—work faster, cheaper, and with fewer side effects.

Sanctions-first is not softness. It is precision. It acts on the levers operators already control: access rights, prices, schedules, and escrowed bonds. It is auditable, reversible, and teachable. It matches the Five Pillars: it protects **Birth/Crew** and **Skill/Reliability** by keeping services running; it preserves **Language/Codec** through transparent rules; it defends **Land/Habitat** with price and capacity discipline; and it enforces **Security (bounded)** by burning bonds and denying slots before anyone reaches for a weapon.

It also keeps faith with the Rails: **Exit & Safe Passage** via green lanes and corridor elasticity; **Non-Hostility & Non-Dispossession** through targeted, bond-priced penalties; **Rescue First** through humanitarian minima baked into every order; **Neutral Courts** via fast Interim Orders; **Open Corridors & Standards** through multi-implementation conformance; and **Leashed Replication** by tying sanctions to signed lineages and rate limits.

2) The Defect Inequality & Bond Pricing
---------------------------------------

Bad actors defect when the expected return outweighs the expected sanction. Write it cleanly:

**Defect if**  $G \times R > p \times S \times L$ .

Where:

*    $G$  = attacker’s gain (net),
*    $R$  = repeatability (how often a tactic can be reused),
*    $p$  = probability of detection/adjudication,
*    $S$  = sanction size (burn + access loss),
*    $L$  = latency between offense and bite.

We design enforcement so the defect condition fails. Our target:

**Design guardrail:**  $G \times R \leq 0.3 \times p \times S \times L$ .

That 0.3 safety factor buys parameter wobble and uncertainty. We increase  $p$  with telemetry proofs and randomized audits; increase  $L$  by shrinking latency (faster IOs, automated execution); increase  $S$  through calibrated bonds and denial ladders; decrease  $G$  and  $R$  by removing choke profits (price caps, AMMs) and rate-limiting replays.

**Bond pricing per right.** For a domain-specific right  $d$ :

$$
S_d = k \cdot \frac{\hat{G} \cdot \hat{R}}{\hat{p} \cdot \hat{L}}, \quad k \ge 3
$$
*   Hats (ˆ) are current best estimates with uncertainty bands.
*    $k$  is the safety margin. We publish  $k$  and its rationale.
*   Auto-escalation doubles  $S_d$  for each adverse adjudication within a 90-day window (cap at 5× pending FO).

Defaults (tune locally; publish your variance bands):

*   **Traffic/Ports:**  $S_{traffic} \approx 9 \times \hat{G}$ 
*   **Replication/Lineage misuse:**  $S_{repl} \approx 5 \times \hat{G}$ 
*   **Cooling/Energy market manipulation:**  $S_{cool} \approx 7 \times \hat{G}$ 

These factors assume  $p \in [0.5,0.8]$  with IO latency ≤72h and automated execution. If your  $p$  or speed is worse, raise  $k$ .

3) Tribunal Architecture (Speed with Due Process)
-------------------------------------------------

We use two tiers. We keep clocks hard. We publish reasons.

**Interim Orders (IO).**

*   SLA: ≤72 hours from filing to order.
*   Action: reversible tranche burn (≤20% of posted bond), provisional slot/port limits, temporary price braces.
*   Evidence: machine-readable bundles (telemetry proofs, price feeds, lineage attestations), signed by two independent verifiers or a quorum engine.
*   Publication: order, reasons, and parameters public; private data minimized and sealed.
*   Reversal: automatic restoration and restitution if FO clears the party.

**Final Orders (FO).**

*   SLA: 14–30 days (per corridor class).
*   Action: confirm/adjust burns, extend or lift access limits, assign trusteeship for assets tied to the rail, set remediation milestones.
*   Review: appeals lane with filing stakes; summary denial for non-reproducible claims.
*   Transparency: public reasons, parameter deltas, and anonymized precedent.

Courts are bond-backed, not budget-starved. Tribunals post their own integrity bond; reversal rates and IO/FO latency are published weekly. This builds  $p$  and  $L$  confidence into the inequality.

4) Ports, Slots, Corridors: The Sanctions Surface
-------------------------------------------------

Sanctions bite where traffic flows and capacity is scarce.

**Ports & Docks.** Docking rights, quay time, beaming windows. Denial ladder applies by card:

*   **Yellow:** inspection + delay penalties; low-priority slots removed.
*   **Orange:** new slots frozen; existing slots reduced by a fixed share (e.g., −40%).
*   **Red:** all but humanitarian minima suspended; trustee schedule takes over.

**Beam/Traffic Slots.** Spectrum or path time slices. Misbehavior triggers automatic throttles and slot auctions with violator excluded for defined epochs.

**Corridors.** Capacity floors, price-slope caps, and commit–reveal maintenance windows are published. Sanctions treat corridors as public rails: we restrict access for violators while preserving green lanes for the public.

**Registries.** Participation in naming, identity, and conformance registries is a privilege. A red card can place a non-compliant operator into trustee-managed service until FO.

**Humanitarian invariants (non-negotiable).**

*   **Rescue First:** SAR channels remain open regardless of card.
*   **Neutral Minima:** emergency medical, food, water, and evacuation lanes are exempt from denial and price caps apply.
*   **No Collective Punishment:** sanctions target rights and entities; civilians keep green-lane access.

5) Cooling Markets & Thermo-Finance
-----------------------------------

Cooling is a rail: without it, lives and systems fail. That invites “thermo squeezes.” We prevent them with rule-bound markets.

**AMM design.**

*   Continuous market for cooling credits (kWh-equivalent of cooling load).
*   **Circuit breakers:** halt price moves beyond ±5% in 15 minutes or ±15% intraday, with staged reopen protocols.
*   **Position limits:** per-entity net long/short ≤2% of 30-day mean load.
*   **Buffers:** strategic reserve sized at 10 days of the 95th-percentile daily load; release rules pre-published and triggered by price/temperature bands.
*   **Commit–reveal maintenance:** operators announce maintenance windows with cryptographic commitments; reveals are checked post-hoc; deviations incur tranche burns.

**Governance.** The AMM and buffers run under the tribunal’s parameter charter. All changes are approved via rotating quorum engines (≥3 implementations), preventing a single vendor or desk from steering the market.

6) Standards as Commons (Not Thrones)
-------------------------------------

Sanctions credibility depends on multi-implementation rails. A single stack is a single point of failure and a single point of capture.

**Rules.**

*   **Plurality:** ≥3 independently developed, certified implementations per critical control plane.
*   **Rotating editors:** standards registries rotate stewardship on a fixed timetable.
*   **Escrowed bug bounties:** bounty funds pre-escrowed; awards executed via tribunal orders.
*   **Grace windows:** compliant actors get bounded time to upgrade (e.g., 30–90 days) when a standard advances; sanctions target deliberate non-conformance, not lag.

Outcome: lower outage variance, higher  $p$  (because telemetry is standardized), and smaller  $G$  (since choke profits collapse when failover exists).

2) The Defect Inequality & Bond Pricing
---------------------------------------

People and firms defect when the expected upside of misbehavior outweighs the expected sanction, discounted by detection and delay. We write it cleanly:

**Defect if**  $G \times R > p \times S \times L$ .

*    $G$  is the attacker’s net gain.
*    $R$  is repeatability—how often a tactic can be replayed before it is obsoleted.
*    $p$  is the probability the act will be detected and adjudicated.
*    $S$  is sanction size—the burn plus the value of lost access.
*    $L$  is latency—the speed from offense to bite.

The engineering goal is to make the defect condition fail with margin:

Boost  $L$  by compressing time from evidence to reversible bite; the shorter the interval, the less value misbehavior can extract. Increase  $S$  by sizing bonds to expected gain and by using access denials with known opportunity costs. Reduce  $G$  by collapsing choke profits (e.g., outlawed spreads and price-slope caps), and reduce  $R$  by making replays hard (rate limits, lineage checks, commit–reveal calendars).

$$
S_d = k \cdot \frac{\hat{G} \cdot \hat{R}}{\hat{p} \cdot \hat{L}}, \quad k \ge 3
