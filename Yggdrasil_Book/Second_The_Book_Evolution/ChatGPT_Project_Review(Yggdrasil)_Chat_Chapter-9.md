## Response:
9 — Replication Governance (RGP) & Lineage
==========================================

1) The Replication Problem (and why it decides everything)
----------------------------------------------------------

Replication—machines printing machines, labs growing systems, models forking models—multiplies capability and risk at the same time. Runaway growth, gray-market forking, and correlated defects can overwhelm corridors, drain cooling and habitat reserves, and destabilize trust. When replication goes wrong:

*   **Land/Habitat** absorbs externalities: heat, waste, invasive organisms, unstable inventories.
*   **Security (bounded)** weakens as uncontrolled assets appear in the wild.
*   **Skill/Reliability** erodes when push-button replication deskills operators without raising audit capacity.
*   **Birth/Crew Formation** suffers when households face hazard spillovers and brittle supply.
*   **Language/Codec** drifts as private standards multiply and public rails fragment.

The Rails exist to protect non-members from someone else’s growth curve: **exit & safe passage**, **non-hostility & non-dispossession**, **rescue first**, **neutral courts**, **open corridors & ≥3-stack standards**, and **leashed replication**. “**Sanctions before shots**” is the operating rule: use ports, slots, bonds, and schedules to shape behavior; reserve force for one purpose only—**to reopen the rails**.

2) Threat Model & Failure Modes
-------------------------------

Map threats concretely so controls match reality:

*   **Unlicensed fab lines:** shadow shifts, cloned tooling, spoofed labels shipped through third-party depots.
*   **Forged provenance:** copied manifests and recycled signatures; “clean” outer crates, dirty core.
*   **Bio gene-drive leakage:** edited organisms with poorly tested reversal pathways, released via “field trials.”
*   **Rogue auto-scaling (digital):** cloud/cluster scripts that treat traffic spikes as license to spawn unvetted replicas.
*   **Vendor monoculture updates:** a pushed firmware/model that doubles replication rate across thousands of devices—one bug, global impact.
*   **Goodhart drift:** teams optimize for output or model wins rather than reliability and corridor SLAs.

Failure shows up as heat/cooling shortages, brittle inventories, tribunal backlogs, and public panic. Panic is the accelerant; it tempts leaders into indefinite emergency. RGP is built to make panic unnecessary.

3) The RGP Core: Throttles, Bonds, Quotas, and Rights
-----------------------------------------------------

RGP sets **knobs** that bind growth to reliability:

*   **Rate limits per right**, not per entity. The right to replicate attaches to **ports/slots/facilities**, each with posted ceilings, not just to organizations. This avoids whack-a-mole shell games.
*   **Bonds sized by enforcement math.** Set sanction **S** using  
    **S ≥ k·(Ĝ·R̂)/(p̂·L̂)**  
    where **Ĝ** = attacker gain (bounded estimate), **R̂** = repeatability, **p̂** = detection probability (audit power), **L̂** = latency to sanction, **k** = safety factor. This keeps **G×R ≤ 0.3×p×S×L** in practice \[see “Adversarial Economics”\].
*   **Quotas for high-externality classes.** Gene drives, auto-replicating agents, and high-TDP fabs publish monthly ceilings tied to cooling and habitat reserves.
*   **Rights/permits are transferable only with lineage proofs.** A slot or permit moves only when its **signed component tree** validates through a recognized conformance suite (**≥3 independent implementations**).
*   **Tribunal clocks.** **Interim Order (IO) ≤72h** for freezes and green-lane carve-outs; **Final Order (FO) ≤14–30d** with public reasons.
*   **Sanctions before shots.** First resorts: **port denial**, **schedule shifts**, **fee uplifts**, **reversible tranche burns** of posted bonds.

4) Lineage: Signed Trees from Blueprint to Bolt
-----------------------------------------------

Lineage is proof that a thing is what it claims to be—without handing over the shop.

*   **Signed component trees:** every assembly references hashed parts and processes; each edge carries a signature from the responsible steward (human-readable role, rotating pseudonymous key).
*   **Build manifests:** reproducible build instructions with version pins and environment hashes.
*   **Model cards & weight hashes:** for digital artifacts, store activation date, training data classes (redacted), evaluation results, and binary fingerprints.
*   **Chain-of-custody logs:** time-stamped handoffs from port to line to shipper to recipient; **commit–reveal** windows for maintenance to frustrate cartel timing.
*   **Teardown lottery:** 1–3% randomized destructive audits. The audit authority buys the item at fair price, tears it down, and publishes a minimal proof (pass/fail + delta notes).
*   **Privacy budget:** minimize data collection; keep raw process telemetry local; export only **salted rotating IDs** and proofs (zero-knowledge where feasible). Lineage verifies; it does not surveil.

5) Biological Parity: Wet Lab RGP
---------------------------------

Apply the same grammar to biology, with domain-specific gates:

*   **Tiered facilities:** BSL-linked access with replication quotas tied to containment class and habitat risk.
*   **Incident escrow:** pre-posted sample/sequence escrow for high-impact work; IO can open escrow to a neutral lab within 72 hours.
*   **Reversibility proofs:** gene drives and self-propagating edits must ship with demonstrated counter-drive or decay plan; **no reversal, no release**.
*   **Consent and scope:** heritable edits require consent regimes and habitat-bound pilots; quarantine zones time-boxed and posted under **Rails** minima (exit, neutral recourse, SAR support).
*   **Parity and dignity:** safeguards apply to **bioforms and synthetics** symmetrically; throttles target externalities, not identity.

6) Digital & AI Replication: Forking as Reproduction
----------------------------------------------------

Treat forks as activation events with custody:

*   **Lineage IDs for forks:** every new model instance or fine-tune emits a lineage child with parent pointers and signed activation.
*   **Habeas Computus minima:** operators must be able to show **what is running where and under whose authority** \[see “Open Corridors & Standards”\].
*   **Two-key hazardous overrides:** for high-impact modules (autonomy, bio-design, power control), require two independent keys and quorum engines to **pause or de-scale**.
*   **Conscience safe-harbor:** engineers may refuse orders that violate the **Rails**; safe harbors are enforceable via neutral courts.
*   **Quota coupling:** auto-scalers bind to posted quotas and cooling credits; a traffic spike cannot silently multiply hazardous replicas.

7) Markets & Mechanisms: Ports, Slots, AMM Cooling, and Scheduling
------------------------------------------------------------------

Enforcement is mostly **market plumbing**:

*   **Ports/slots:** access is the lever. Non-compliant actors lose dock times, throughput, and storage windows; compliant actors get priority lanes.
*   **Fee uplifts and schedule denial:** price externalities via posted formulas; denial is reversible when proofs arrive.
*   **Cooling AMM with circuit breakers:** allocate cooling/energy credits via an automated market maker; when volatility spikes, **circuit breakers** throttle replication rates until the pool stabilizes.
*   **Commit–reveal maintenance:** publish maintenance windows in hash commitments; reveal 24–72 hours before execution to prevent collusive throttling.
*   **Interoperability:** at least **three independent stacks** implement ports, manifests, and audit proofs; rotating editors prevent capture.

8) Audits That Work: Randomized Teardowns & Honey Purchases
-----------------------------------------------------------

Audit power keeps **p̂** high and **L̂** low.

*   **Cadence:** routine audits at posted percentages; **triggered** audits on anomaly signals; **lottery teardowns** at 1–3%.
*   **Honey-buyer program:** purchase items anonymously to test lineage claims; bounty paid upon verified defect.
*   **Reproducibility bounties:** reward third parties for replicating published builds and exposing divergence.
*   **Tribunal SLAs:** IO ≤72h (freeze, green lanes, data escrow); FO ≤14–30d with reasons and tranche-burn schedule.
*   **Uncertainty bands:** every metric—detection rate, false-positive rate, backlog estimates—published with uncertainty and sensitivity analysis.

9) Privacy & Data Minimization (keeping p̂ high without a panopticon)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

You can verify without voyeurism:

*   **Telemetry floors/ceilings:** collect enough to prove; outlaw surplus.
*   **Rotating salted IDs:** refresh keys on a cadence; bind to role not person; hold mapping under escrow.

1) The Replication Problem (and why it decides everything)
----------------------------------------------------------

Replication—machines printing machines, labs growing systems, models forking models—pushes capability forward and risk sideways. The problem surface is simple to state:

*   **Runaway growth** can overrun habitat, cooling, and corridor schedules faster than operators can respond.
*   **Gray-market forking** and forged provenance bury defects until they fail in the field.
*   **Correlated defects** from monoculture updates propagate globally at machine speed.

When replication misfires, it harms each **Pillar**: **Land/Habitat** (externalities and waste heat), **Security (bounded)** (uncontrolled assets), **Skill/Reliability** (deskilling without audit capacity), **Birth/Crew** (household risk), **Language/Codec** (standards drift). The **Rails** exist to protect non-members from someone else’s growth curve: exit & safe passage, non-hostility & non-dispossession, rescue first, neutral courts with fast clocks, open corridors & ≥3-stack standards, and leashed replication. Enforcement defaults to **sanctions before shots**: ports, slots, bonds, fee/schedule controls—force only to **reopen the rails**.

* * *

2) Threat Model & Failure Modes
-------------------------------

A workable governance regime starts with concrete adversaries and failure shapes:

*   **Unlicensed fab lines.** Night shifts, cloned tooling, spoofed labels routed through legit depots.
*   **Forged provenance.** Clean crates, dirty cores; manifests recycled across lots.
*   **Gene-drive leakage.** Field trials without counter-drive or decay plans.
*   **Rogue auto-scaling.** Cloud/cluster scripts that treat traffic spikes as license to multiply unvetted replicas.
*   **Vendor monoculture “push.”** A firmware/model push that doubles replication across thousands—one bug, global blast radius.
*   **Goodhart drift.** Teams optimize for output or leaderboard wins instead of corridor SLAs and reliability.

Symptoms show up as cooling crunches, throughput variance, tribunal backlogs, and public panic. Panic is the accelerant; **RGP** is designed to make panic unnecessary.

3) The RGP Core: Throttles, Bonds, Quotas, and Rights
-----------------------------------------------------

RGP provides a few simple, enforceable knobs:

**Rate limits attach to rights, not entities.** The right to replicate lives on **ports, slots, and facilities** with posted ceilings. Permits are scarce, auditable, and divisible; shell-games by ownership changes don’t matter.

**Bonds price the defect inequality.** For any class, post sanction **S** using:

**S ≥ k·(Ĝ·R̂)/(p̂·L̂)**

*   **Ĝ**: bounded estimate of violator gain per incident
*   **R̂**: repeatability of the violation (0–1)
*   **p̂**: detection probability (0–1)
*   **L̂**: latency to sanction (scale to 1/day)
*   **k**: safety factor

This implements the practical constraint **G×R ≤ 0.3×p×S×L** \[see _Adversarial Economics_\]. Publish **k** by class and keep the rest internal.

**Quotas cap high-externality replication.** Gene drives, self-replicating agents, and high-TDP lines post monthly ceilings and renewal rules tied to cooling/habitat reserves.

**Rights are transferable only with lineage proofs.** A slot moves only when its **signed component tree** validates through an open conformance suite with **≥3 independent implementations**.

**Tribunal clocks.** **Interim Order (IO) ≤72h** for freezes and green lanes; **Final Order (FO) ≤14–30d** with public reasons and proportionate sanctions.

**Sanctions ladder.** Port denial → schedule reshapes → fee uplifts → **reversible tranche burns** of posted bonds. Force is last—used only to reopen a corridor.

* * *

4) Lineage: Signed Trees from Blueprint to Bolt
-----------------------------------------------

Lineage proves “this thing is what it claims to be”—without forcing anyone to publish trade secrets.

**Signed component trees.** Every assembly references hashed parts, processes, and test artifacts. Each edge is signed by a responsible steward using a rotating pseudonymous key with role metadata.

**Build manifests.** Reproducible instructions with version pins, environment hashes, and differential tests. For digital artifacts, **model cards** record training data classes (redacted), evaluation results, and **weight hashes**.

**Chain-of-custody.** Time-stamped handoffs from port to line to shipper to recipient. Maintenance uses **commit–reveal** windows to defeat cartel timing.

**Teardown lottery.** Post a **1–3%** destructive audit rate. Buy the item at fair price, tear it down, publish a minimal proof (pass/fail and delta locations). Rotate auditors.

**Privacy budget.** Keep raw process telemetry local; export only **salted rotating IDs** and proofs (zero-knowledge where feasible). Lineage verifies; it does not surveil.

6) Digital & AI Replication: Forking as Reproduction

**Two-key hazardous overrides.** For autonomy, bio-design, or power-control modules, use a quorum engine and two independent keys to **pause or de-scale**.

**Quota coupling.** Auto-scalers bind to posted quotas and cooling credits; traffic spikes cannot silently multiply hazardous replicas.

**Conscience safe-harbor.** Engineers can refuse orders that violate the Rails; neutral courts enforce that right.
