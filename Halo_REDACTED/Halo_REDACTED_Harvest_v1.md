# Halo: REDACTED — Harvest

Sep 25, 2026 · @Julien Appleby-Millette

Investment proposal to the Activision greenlight committee and Microsoft leadership.

## Executive summary

Halo: REDACTED makes Halo a two-pillar franchise. It is a premium co-op war game set in humanity's first war with the Covenant, and it ships ahead of the next Master Chief epic: it earns on every platform, returns Halo to its roots as a military industrial fantasy game, and gives the Chief's return a proven foundation and a rested audience. We ask for $34M to run 18 months of preproduction at Core scale, leading into a $195M production.

- **The Master Chief line needs rest.** The Paramount+ series turned the Chief into a punchline: his bare-backside scene and a sex scene with a prisoner revived the "Master Cheeks" meme ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/master-cheeks)), and his own actor called the scene "a huge mistake" ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2024/01/19/halo-tv-series-master-chief-actor-sex-scene-huge-mistake/)). Younger players now meet the Chief as a meme; longtime fans are tired of watching the Chief and Cortana mishandled. REDACTED opens a new front without touching either.

### The product

Players fight the war the official record left out: the five-year campaign for the colony world Harvest that cost the UNSC more than half its navy's fighting strength, told through the archive of the Office of Naval Intelligence (ONI). The war runs across seven eras, from first contact to the planet's final glassing, at three scales: four-player Strike Ops, Battlefronts of up to 64 players and hundreds-strong Grand Theaters from the ground to low orbit. Under the shooter runs a live war economy directed by AI orchestrators, the same military-logistics design Halo began with in 1997 as a strategy game ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Development_of_Halo:_Combat_Evolved)). Forge, Halo's player-creation tool, ships at launch, and the best community missions enter the live war.

### Why it wins

REDACTED is built on the pattern shared by the category's hits: squad play, one tight core loop, a premium price, a strong identity and story-driven seasons. It adds two advantages neither Helldivers 2 nor ARC Raiders launched with: a globally known franchise and Activision's marketing reach, on all three platforms from day one. It draws on five teams only Activision holds together: Call of Duty's gunplay, Warzone's scale, Zombies' co-op design, World's Edge's strategy simulation and Rare's shared-world experience.

## The vision

**Halo began as a strategy game about an army at war. REDACTED takes it back there.** Bungie started Halo in 1997 as a real-time strategy game on its Myth engine, with Warthogs and Marines deployed across open battlefields. By its public reveal at Macworld in July 1999 it had become a third-person action game, and after Microsoft bought Bungie it shipped in 2001 as a first-person shooter ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Development_of_Halo:_Combat_Evolved)). Halo's army still feels like a real military because it was designed as a force to command before it became an arsenal to carry. REDACTED puts the strategy game back under the shooter: a live war economy that every player fights inside.

**The war ONI buried.** Harvest is where humanity met the Covenant. The colony went dark in 2525, Preston Cole's fleet barely won back orbit in 2526, and a five-year ground and orbital campaign ran until early 2531; the Covenant returned in 2534 to glass the planet for good. Canon puts the campaign's cost at more than half of the UNSC Navy's fighting strength ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Harvest_campaign)) and never shows where that strength went. It records an ONI prowler watching the frigate *Prophecy* die without helping, and an unidentified Spartan-II killed on Harvest. REDACTED shows the rest: the unreported battlegroups, prototype weapons and Spartan-II operations that the Office of Naval Intelligence (ONI) erased.

**Three tiers of truth.** The game is presented as ONI's Harvest archive, and every piece of it carries a clearance tier. *Verified* is canon: its events, ships and people. *Public Record* is ONI propaganda: inflated victories and recruiting broadcasts. *Redacted* is what players live: secret battlegroups, prototypes and extra Spartan-II operations. When something is fun but breaks canon, ONI classifies it and play continues. Every invented asset is destroyed by 2534, which is why later canon never mentions it. The frame lets the studio invent freely without breaking the franchise's history.

**One war, played at every scale.**

&#91;embedded content: The war simulation · 3 AI orchestrators, 1 economy, 3 play scales, Forge feeding the mission pool\]

Three AI orchestrators run the war. FLEETCOM runs UNSC logistics, Covenant command lands forces, digs for the relic and glasses cities, and ONI Section III scuttles prototypes and covers tracks, sometimes against FLEETCOM. Together they read a simulated supply network, find where it is under stress and publish missions that answer those problems at the scale each one needs. Results write back: a convoy saved in a Strike Op raises supply in a sector, which shapes the Battlefront fought there, which moves territory in the Grand Theater. AI troops fill every battle, so no battle waits for a full server, and a problem nobody takes gets worse on its own.

**Seven eras, each a live war.** The story campaign walks through seven eras in order. Once unlocked, each era also runs as its own live war theater with its own arsenal, enemies and canon anchors. All eras reset together each season, and each era's result seeds the next, so a community's war carries its map, its losses and its glass forward from Contact to Ashfall over about a year.

| Era | Years | What players fight through |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I · Contact | 2525 | The colony falls; militia evacuation |
| II · Retribution | 2525–26 | Cole's fleet wins back orbit |
| III · Liberation | 2526–27 | Ground retaken; war industry built |
| IV · Commitment | 2527–29 | Peak investment and prototypes |
| V · Glassfall | 2529–30 | Mass glassing; fighting retreat |
| VI · Relic | 2530–31 | *Spirit of Fire* arrives; the relic is seized |
| VII · Ashfall | 2531–34 | Final glassing; the archive burns |

**Fun over balance; canon sets the anchors, players shape everything between.** UNSC players stay overpowered at every tier, and enemies never become bullet sponges. Difficulty comes from distance to support, enemy density and objective complexity, so a lost deep mission feels like "we spent the orbital strike too early," never "the enemy was too strong." Death is expected, and drop pods make it a spectacle. Players write the tactical ledger with no limits; the story writes the strategic one. Every player victory raises FLEETCOM's confidence, which sends more ships and industry to Harvest, which draws more Covenant forces, which makes the collapse bigger. The community lives through the same false confidence that doomed the real campaign.

**War states are the content engine.** Helldivers 2 already proves the model: its Game Master chooses the Major Orders and where enemies attack, while "the war fought and liberation of planets — that's all you" ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/helldivers-2-director-explains-the-limits-of-joel-the-game-masters-power)). Its major turns are set, and players meet them as they arrive. REDACTED works the same way. Canon fixes where Harvest's war ends; the orchestrators decide when and how each turn lands; players decide everything between: which cities stand, which supply lines hold, which prototypes survive and how much FLEETCOM commits. Those war states carry into the next era and change its maps, arsenals, enemies and missions, so no two lineages play alike. Arrowhead's CEO has said he would "build automation on the gamemaster" to keep Helldivers 2's war running indefinitely ([GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/third-person-shooter/helldivers-2-ceo-wants-the-galactic-war-to-last-forever-even-if-arrowhead-has-to-sunset-the-game-in-the-far-future-and-the-devs-have-to-make-an-ai-game-master/)). REDACTED starts there.

**Progression grows wide, not tall.** Veterans earn more options, not bigger numbers, so a new player can answer any call for help. Core weapons and vehicles unlock per era; Procurement Contracts from canon manufacturers work like Warbonds and never expire; ONI Black Budget prototypes unlock through field use and clearance and are never for sale. The catalogues tell the story too: contracts multiply through the buildup, and in Glassfall every one is stamped "Procurement terminated."

**PvP stays small and dramatic.** The only way to play the Covenant is as an invader: a commander who drops a small AI lance into a hard mission, limited per operation. The invaded squad can call a quick reaction force, and other players answer by Pelican or ODST drop pod. The UNSC is favored to win, and the rescue becomes the story players tell.

**Players build the war too.** Forge ships at launch with map, mission and scenario tools built on the studio's own toolset. Approved community missions are tagged by the problem they solve, and when the war hits that problem the orchestrators can deploy them. Creators are credited in the archive, and Wargames hosts anything-goes custom games, PvP included, outside canon.

**It holds up when players leave**** ****a****n****d**** e****x****p****a****n****d****s**** ****w****hen th****ey come b****ack****.** Most live-service games have one minimum population, and below it they close. REDACTED has five operating modes, each needing fewer players than the one above:

Live-operations cost falls as the game steps down while expansions keep selling, and the game never has to be switched off. ## The franchise platform

**Harvest is the product; the platform is the upside.** Core scale launches Harvest with Eras I–IV, one Grand Theater region, and Map and Mission Forge. Eras V–VII arrive in the first live year and complete the war. From year two, a paid theater expansion each year opens new ground at $20.

&#91;embedded content: Platform path · Harvest's eras and expansions, the self-funding gate, new wars, and pipeline reuse for the Master Chief line\]

**The win condition is a live war that pays for its own growth.** The live war funds itself when its owners spend more than it costs to run:

`N × (s + a · p · f) ≥ C_live`

Here N is the number of owners, s is net in-game spend per owner per year, a is the expansion attach rate, p is the $20 expansion price, f is the 82% net share after platform fees, and C\_live is about $51M a year at Core scale: a 180-person live team plus servers. On Base-case terms in year two, s ≈ $4.60 and a · p · f ≈ $3.30, so each owner brings about $7.90 and the war funds itself at about 6.5M owners. On Target-case terms each owner brings about $13.50, and the line falls to about 3.8M owners.

Above that line every expansion is profit. Mode 1 runs at full strength, with extra shards added as demand requires, and a full war is the signal to fund the next theater. The same economy, orchestrators, arsenals and Forge tools then carry to other wars in Halo's history: Reach before its fall, the Insurrection, the Halo rings and other settings. Each new war is a content project on a proven platform, not a new game.

**The spend builds the next Master Chief game's pipeline.** Activision has given its new Halo team one goal: "make the greatest Halo game ever" ([GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/activision-prepares-to-make-the-greatest-halo-game-ever-as-it-takes-control-of-the-franchise-from-halo-studios/)). REDACTED is how that team gets there. It rebuilds Halo's vehicles, weapons, environments and engine work inside Activision's pipeline, and about 15% of its development value, $29M at Core scale, carries straight into the Master Chief game that follows.

**The proving ground returns live answers.** Before the Chief returns, REDACTED shows Microsoft, with its own player data:

- that Activision's team builds Halo combat longtime fans accept;
- that a new Halo game sells on PlayStation and Steam as well as Xbox;
- which eras, vehicles, weapons and enemies players spend their time with;
- that players come back to Halo while the Chief rests.

The next Master Chief game then launches on proven tools, a tested team and an audience that has been playing Halo for years.

And because its war is a simulation run by AI agents, REDACTED gives Microsoft's gameplay AI research, including Muse ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/introducing-muse-our-first-generative-ai-model-designed-for-gameplay-ideation/)), a flagship production to prove itself on.

**The returns.**

&#91;embedded content: REDACTED budget model, Ceiling sheet · Core scale, conservative agent scenario · present value over five years at 10%\]

On Base-case terms, each copy sold is worth about $47 in five-year present value once in-game spending and expansions are counted. At Core scale that gives:

`copies needed = C_attributed × m ÷ (PV per copy) = $334M × 1.5 ÷ $47 ≈ 10.6M`

- **Pays back at about 7.1M copies**, when present value equals the $334M attributed cost.
- **Clears a 1.5× return at 10.6M copies**, about half of Helldivers 2's sales.
- **Base case, 8M copies:** $379M present value, 1.1× attributed cost.
- **Target case, 20M copies (Helldivers 2's result):** $1.24B present value, 3.7× attributed cost and 3.4× the all-in spend.

The Downside case, 3M copies, returns $111M. Preproduction is built to catch that outcome early: a downside surfaces at the preproduction gate for $34M, not after $334M.

**Agents lower the bar further.** The budget above uses the conservative agent-assisted scenario, which saves $12M on Core development. The target scenario saves $31M, cuts Core development to $176M and lowers the copies needed to 9.6M.

## Execution plan

**Four years from approval to launch, and every production dollar follows proof.** Preproduction runs 18 months at Core scale and ends in a production decision. Production runs 30 months to a simultaneous launch on PC, PlayStation 5 and Xbox. The first live year completes Harvest's war.

&#91;embedded content: Program plan · 3 phases, 3 preproduction gates, 3 production milestones, months from approval\]

**Preproduction builds the riskiest things first.** Each gate releases the next tranche of the $34M only when its criteria are met.

| Gate | Month | What it proves | Pass criteria | Tranche released |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gate 1: Core loop | 6 | Strike Ops generated by a live war economy are fun to replay | Orchestrator v1 publishes Strike Ops in Era I and Era V; 8 of 10 external playtest squads choose to replay | $11M for months 6–12 |
| Gate 2: Vertical slice | 12 | The game works at every scale, and war states diverge | One Harvest region with Strike Ops and a Battlefront; a Grand Theater technical test holds its target player count within the server budget per player-hour; lineages seeded with different Era I outcomes produce clearly different maps, arsenals, enemies and mission mixes in the next era | $16M for months 12–18 |
| Gate 3: Production go | 18 | The program is ready to scale | Production budget confirmed within 10% of plan; lead and support studios staffed by name; demand sized by platform; overlap with Call of Duty measured | Production at Core scale |

The first $7M funds months 0–6 on approval.

**Preproduction answers seven questions,** each feeding a gate:

1. **Outcomes:** the full comparable-launch record, extended beyond the 14 titles in the appendix. Feeds Gate 3.
2. **Demand by platform:** who buys, on which store, and how strongly Master Chief fatigue shapes interest in a Chief-free Halo. Feeds Gate 3.
3. **War-state variance:** how far each era's outcome changes the maps, arsenals, enemies and missions of the next, across thousands of agent-simulated lineages and the playtest lineages. Feeds Gate 2.
4. **Population:** which operating mode the game should launch in. Feeds Gate 3.
5. **Budget and staffing:** the bottom-up production budget and studio plan. Feeds Gate 3.
6. **Grand Theater cost:** server cost per player-hour at full scale. Feeds Gate 2.
7. **Portfolio overlap:** how much of REDACTED's revenue is new to Activision. Feeds Gate 3.

**The team scales with the gates.** Preproduction opens with a lead team of about 40 from Activision's new Halo team and grows to about 140 by month 18. Production follows Call of Duty's lead-and-support model: the lead team owns the sandbox, the orchestrators and Grand Theater technology, and support studios each own era theaters, peaking at about 330 people. Halo Studios reviews canon throughout, World's Edge advises on the war economy, and Rare advises on the shared world and invasions.

**Agents are in the pipeline from month one.** The orchestrators are AI agents, so the same simulation plays thousands of wars overnight to tune the economy, difficulty bands and mission mix before a human playtest. Human playtests then focus on what agents cannot judge: feel, fun and story.

## Risk management

**The plan puts the largest risks in front of the smallest spend.** Every major risk is tested in preproduction, where $34M is at stake, before production commits the remaining $161M of development and $117M of marketing.

&#91;embedded content: Risk map · 8 program risks, placed by the team's assessment of likelihood and impact\]

| Risk | Why it matters | How REDACTED handles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch underperforms | Of 14 comparable premium co-op and live-service launches since 2018, 8 failed or were written off (appendix) | Built on the winners' pattern: squad play, one core loop, a $40 price. Money is released gate by gate, so a weak result surfaces at $7M–$34M |
| War states converge | Lineages that end in similar states would play alike | War states carry forward and reshape the next era's maps, arsenals, enemies and missions; agents simulate thousands of lineages to widen that variance, and the war-state test runs at Gate 2 |
| Grand Theater cost or stability | Hundreds of players from ground to orbit is the most demanding system | Built after the core loop; technical test and server cost per player-hour at Gate 2; if over budget, Grand Theaters launch in one region and expand live |
| Thin population | Seven eras can split players | Launch in Mode 2 or 3, eras merge, AI troops fill every battle; the population study sets the launch mode at Gate 3 |
| PC and PlayStation demand | Halo's history is on Xbox | Campaign Evolved sold 41% of its copies on PlayStation; the demand study sizes every platform at Gate 3 |
| Fan trust in the handover | Longtime fans are wary after Infinite and the series | Halo Studios reviews canon; the Chief and Cortana stay off-screen; ONI Black Budget power is never for sale |
| Team capacity | Activision's teams carry Call of Duty | A dedicated Halo team leads; support studios staffed by name before Gate 3 |
| Call of Duty overlap | Shared shooter audience | Different mode, setting, price and cadence; the portfolio study measures overlap at Gate 3 |

**Two further protections run for the life of the game.** The five-mode ladder means REDACTED never reaches a population cliff and never has to be switched off, which also meets the direction of the Stop Killing Games initiative. And because the platform's assets carry into later Halo games, even a modest result pays forward into the franchise.

## Appendix

### A. Budget model

All budget and return figures come from the REDACTED budget model workbook. Costs are built from headcount by phase:

- **Labor:** a blended $199.5K per person-year, from $240K at the lead studio, $180K at support studios and $90K for outsourcing, split 55/30/15. The lead-studio rate is derived from Spider-Man 2's roughly $300M cost and 380 peak staff ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/what-hacked-files-tell-us-about-the-studio-behind-spide-1851115233)).
- **Non-labor costs:** 15% of labor, for tools, middleware, motion capture, hardware and licensing.
- **Contingency:** 15%. Spider-Man 2 overran its $270M budget by about 11%.
- **Marketing:** 60% of development, below Raph Koster's AAA benchmark of 75–100% ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-cost-of-games)) because Activision and Xbox own major channels.
- **Phases:** 18 months of preproduction, 30 of production and a first live year.
- **Servers:** $8M, $18M and $35M in the first live year for Lean, Core and Full; the Grand Theater cost study replaces these with cost per player-hour.
- **Franchise reuse credit:** 15% of development, carried into later Halo titles.

Revenue is valued over five years:

`PV = Σ (t = 1…5) [ c_t · n + O_t · s · (1 − d)^(t−1) · f + O_t · a · p · f · [t ≥ 2] ] ÷ (1 + r)^(t−1)`

Here c\_t is copies sold in year t (55%, 20%, 12%, 8% and 5% of lifetime copies), n is $28.60 net per copy, O\_t is cumulative owners, s is in-game spend per owner in year one, d is the 30% annual decline in that spend, f is the 82% net share, a is the expansion attach rate, p is the $20 expansion price and r is the 10% discount rate. The copies needed to justify a scale follow from `copies needed = C_attributed × m ÷ (PV ÷ copies)`, with m = 1.5.

| Case | Lifetime copies | Spend per owner, year one | Expansion attach | Five-year PV | Budget ceiling at 1.5× |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Downside | 3M | $4 | 10% | $111M | $74M |
| Base | 8M | $8 | 20% | $379M | $253M |
| Target | 20M | $15 | 30% | $1,244M | $830M |

Net per copy starts from Helldivers 2's realized price of about $35 ($700M+ on 20M+ copies, [Alinea Analytics](https://alineaanalytics.substack.com/p/helldivers-2-sells-20m-copies)) and applies Valve's tiered revenue share ([Variety](https://variety.com/2018/gaming/news/valve-revenue-split-changes-1203078700/)) and console platform fees at a 50/25/25 Steam, PlayStation and Xbox mix. The ARC Raiders benchmark for in-game spend is derived from Nexon's Q2 2026 results ([Nexon](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260813331960/en/Nexon-Releases-Earnings-for-Second-Quarter-2026)).

### B. Agent-assisted development

The budget applies a saving to each discipline's eligible work, at 80% adoption:

| Discipline | Conservative saving | Target saving | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Game design and balancing | 10% | 30% | Agent simulation tunes the war economy; 38% of developers use agents for balancing ([Google Cloud](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2025-08-18-90-of-Games-Developers-Already-Using-AI-in-Workflows)) |

| Online, server and simulation engineering | 10% | 25% | Orchestrator logic suits simulation-driven testing |
| Art and animation | 0% | 5% | See the note below |
| Audio | 0% | 5% | Human-led |
| Narrative and cinematics | 0% | 0% | Human-led; canon-sensitive |
| QA and playtesting | 25% | 45% | Automated agent playtesting; 47% of developers use AI in playtesting and balancing ([Google Cloud](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2025-08-18-90-of-Games-Developers-Already-Using-AI-in-Workflows)) |
| Production and management | 5% | 10% | Scheduling, reporting and documentation |
| Live operations and community | 10% | 25% | Telemetry, scheduling, localization support |

The weighted saving is 5.8% of development in the conservative scenario, or $12M at Core scale, and $31M in the target scenario.

**Note on player-facing art.** The conservative scenario takes no agent saving on player-facing art or audio. Black Ops 7's suspected AI-generated art drew public criticism ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/call-of-duty-black-ops-7/ai-assets-steam-disclosure)), while agent-assisted workflows and the quality of generated 3D assets and textures are improving quickly. The art policy is set at Gate 3 against the tools and player expectations of that time.

### E. The confidence trap

FLEETCOM's commitment to Harvest grows faster than the victories that justify it:

`A_(k+1) = A_k + α · I_k^p,  with α > 0 and p > 1`

A\_k is the UNSC force committed in era k, and I\_k is the community's success in that era. Because p > 1, early victories compound FLEETCOM's commitment; Covenant pressure rises in response, and the authored collapse at Glassfall destroys whatever has been committed. The better players do, the larger the loss they live through.
