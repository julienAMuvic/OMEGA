# Halo: REDACTED — Harvest

Sep 25, 2026 · @Julien Appleby-Millette

Investment proposal to the Activision greenlight committee and Microsoft leadership.

## Executive summary

Halo: REDACTED is our proposal for the next Halo game. It is a premium co-op war game set in humanity's first war with the Covenant: it earns on every platform, returns Halo to its roots as a game about an army at war, and gives Activision's new Halo team a full-feature mainline game in a category that has already proven its winners. We ask for $34M to run 18 months of preproduction. Preproduction ends in a production decision of $136M for the $50 Battlefront edition, or $161M for the $60 Theater edition if Grand Theaters pass their Gate 2 test.

### The opportunity

- **The category is large and paying out.** Co-op games earned over $8B on Steam in 2025, up 9% ([Alinea Analytics](https://alineaanalytics.substack.com/p/helldivers-2-sells-20m-copies)). Helldivers 2 has sold 20M+ copies for over $700M; ARC Raiders has sold 16.3M ([Nexon](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260813331960/en/Nexon-Releases-Earnings-for-Second-Quarter-2026)). Both are squad games; Warhammer 40,000: Space Marine 2 showed the same formula sells at $60 on a strong license.
- **Halo is at a turning point.** Activision now steers the franchise ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/activision-taking-over-halo-franchise-and-what-remains-of-halo-studios-will-now-be-a-support-team-2000736427)), Halo now ships on PlayStation, Xbox and PC, and Xbox content and services revenue fell 10% in the June 2026 quarter ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast)). Activision has committed to a "full-feature, mainline installment" and has named no story, protagonist, genre or engine for it ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/activision-halo-game-everything-know-140439377.html)). REDACTED proposes what that game should be.
- **The next Halo game does not need the Chief.** The Paramount+ series was cancelled after two seasons ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/paramount-has-cancelled-its-halo-show-after-2-seasons/)) and turned the Chief into a meme ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/master-cheeks)); Halo Infinite killed Cortana off-screen ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/halo-infinite-ending)). A Chief-free Halo has sold at franchise scale before: Halo 3: ODST sold 2.5M copies in its first two weeks ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/halo-3-odst-drops-25-million-halo-reach-confirmed-as-prequel/1100-6232258/)). REDACTED opens a new front and leaves the Chief's story untouched for whenever Activision chooses to tell it.

### The product

Players fight the war the official record left out: the five-year campaign for the colony world Harvest that cost the UNSC more than half its navy's fighting strength, told through the archive of the Office of Naval Intelligence (ONI). The war runs across seven eras, from first contact to the planet's final glassing. It takes the Helldivers formula and extends it two ways. **Battlefronts** put up to 64 players into one fight with Halo's full vehicle roster: Warthogs, Scorpions, Hornets and Pelicans against Ghosts, Banshees, Wraiths and Scarabs. **Invasions** let a player command a Covenant strike into another squad's mission, and other players drop in to answer. Four-player Strike Ops carry the core loop. Hundreds-strong **Grand Theaters** from the ground to low orbit are an optional third scale, tested at Gate 2; if they ship, the game launches at $60, and if not, at $50. Under the shooter runs a live war economy directed by AI orchestrators, the same military-logistics design Halo began with in 1997 as a strategy game ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Development_of_Halo:_Combat_Evolved)). Forge, Halo's player-creation tool, ships at launch, and the best community missions enter the live war.

### Why it wins

REDACTED is built on the pattern shared by the category's hits: squad play, one tight core loop, a premium price, a strong identity and story-driven seasons. It adds what neither Helldivers 2 nor ARC Raiders launched with: combined-arms Battlefronts on Halo's vehicle sandbox, player-commanded invasions, a globally known franchise and creator tools from launch, backed by Activision's marketing reach. It is built on the Unreal Engine 5 pipeline Halo Studios shipped Halo: Campaign Evolved on, and it draws on five teams only Activision holds together: Call of Duty's gunplay, Warzone's scale, Zombies' co-op design, World's Edge's strategy simulation and Rare's shared-world experience.

At the Base case of 8M copies, the planning case, both editions roughly return their cost (1.00× and 0.98×); a breakout returns about twice it. The $10 step to the Theater edition roughly pays for Grand Theaters and no more, which is why they are optional: they ship only if Gate 2 shows they hold their player count within the server budget.

**The price is set by the game, not the category.** A $50 Battlefront edition keeps its value over a $40 price unless the higher price costs more than about 15% of copies. The demand study tests that directly before production.

**Spend per owner matters as much as copies.** At Base-case in-game spend ($8 per owner in year one, 20% expansion attach) the Battlefront edition pays back at 8.0M copies; at Target-case spend ($15, 30%) it pays back at 6.2M. Procurement Contracts, theater expansions and the war's seasonal pull are first-order design targets, sized in preproduction.

**The spend builds Halo's production pipeline under Activision.** REDACTED brings Halo's vehicles, weapons, environments and engine work into Activision's workflows, and every later Halo game inherits them. About 15% of development value, $26M–$29M, carries into future Halo titles.

**AI agents build and test it for less.** The war economy is itself a simulation run by AI agents, so the same agents playtest the game, balance its economy before players arrive and speed the move of existing Halo assets into Activision's tools. The budget takes the conservative agent-assisted saving; Gate 2 measures the realized saving before production is budgeted on it. The program also gives Microsoft a flagship showcase for agent-assisted production.

### The upside

Harvest is the product; the REDACTED platform is the upside. Once the live war holds a population large enough to make new theater expansions profitable, the same engine and systems extend to other eras of Halo's history: the Halo rings, Reach before its fall and the Insurrection, and other settings. None of that upside is in the numbers above.

### The ask

Approve $34M for 18 months of preproduction, released in three tranches against gates. Preproduction delivers a playable vertical slice, the edition decision at Gate 2, a confirmed production budget, a staffing plan and a demand model for price, copies and spend per owner. On meeting its targets, the program moves into a 30-month production for launch roughly four years from approval.

## The vision

**Halo began as a strategy game about an army at war. REDACTED takes it back there.** Bungie started Halo in 1997 as a real-time strategy game on its Myth engine, with Warthogs and Marines deployed across open battlefields. By its public reveal at Macworld in July 1999 it had become a third-person action game, and after Microsoft bought Bungie it shipped in 2001 as a first-person shooter ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Development_of_Halo:_Combat_Evolved)). Halo's army still feels like a real military because it was designed as a force to command before it became an arsenal to carry. REDACTED puts the strategy game back under the shooter: a live war economy that every player fights inside.

**The war ONI buried.** Harvest is where humanity met the Covenant. The colony went dark in 2525, Preston Cole's fleet barely won back orbit in 2526, and a five-year ground and orbital campaign ran until early 2531; the Covenant returned in 2534 to glass the planet for good. Canon puts the campaign's cost at more than half of the UNSC Navy's fighting strength ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Harvest_campaign)) and never shows where that strength went. It records an ONI prowler watching the frigate *Prophecy* die without helping, and an unidentified Spartan-II killed on Harvest. REDACTED shows the rest: the unreported battlegroups, prototype weapons and Spartan-II operations that ONI erased.

**Three tiers of truth.** The game is presented as ONI's Harvest archive, and every piece of it carries a clearance tier. *Verified* is canon: its events, ships and people. *Public Record* is ONI propaganda: inflated victories and recruiting broadcasts. *Redacted* is what players live: secret battlegroups, prototypes and extra Spartan-II operations. When something is fun but breaks canon, ONI classifies it and play continues. Every invented asset is destroyed by 2534, which is why later canon never mentions it. The frame lets the studio invent freely without breaking the franchise's history.

**One war, played at every scale.**

&#91;embedded content: The war simulation · 3 AI orchestrators, 1 economy, Strike Ops and Battlefronts with the optional Grand Theater scale, Forge feeding the mission pool\]

Three AI orchestrators run the war. Each is a planning agent over the supply-network simulation: it reads the war state, scores candidate missions against its faction's objectives and publishes the best. They choose and schedule; they do not generate player-facing art, dialogue or canon, which stay human-made. FLEETCOM runs UNSC logistics, Covenant command lands forces, digs for the relic and glasses cities, and ONI Section III scuttles prototypes and covers tracks, sometimes against FLEETCOM. Together they read a simulated supply network, find where it is under stress and publish missions that answer those problems at the scale each one needs. Results write back: a convoy saved in a Strike Op raises supply in a sector, which shapes the Battlefront fought there, which moves the front line across the region, and, in the Theater edition, territory in the Grand Theater. AI troops fill every battle, so no battle waits for a full server, and a problem nobody takes gets worse on its own.

**Battlefronts are where Halo's sandbox goes to war.** Helldivers 2 gives four players a stratagem menu; REDACTED gives up to 64 players a motor pool. A Battlefront is a combined-arms operation on one sector of Harvest: Warthog convoys run supply under Banshee cover, Scorpion platoons hold the line against Wraith artillery, Hornets and Pelicans move squads between objectives, and a Scarab walking through a colony town is a problem the whole server has to solve. The Harvest-era roster is canonical, from *Halo Wars*' UNSC and Covenant forces of 2531, and the orchestrators decide which vehicles a sector's supply can field. Losing a convoy in a Strike Op means fewer Scorpions in the Battlefront that follows.

**Seven eras, each a live war.** The story campaign walks through seven eras in order. Once unlocked, each era also runs as its own live war theater with its own arsenal, enemies and canon anchors. Seasons run about seven weeks. At each reset every era's result seeds the era after it, so one community's war moves an era forward each season and carries its map, its losses and its glass from Contact to Ashfall in about a year.

| Era | Years | What players fight through |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I · Contact | 2525 | The colony falls; militia evacuation |
| II · Retribution | 2525–26 | Cole's fleet wins back orbit |
| III · Liberation | 2526–27 | Ground retaken; war industry built |
| IV · Commitment | 2527–29 | Peak investment and prototypes |
| V · Glassfall | 2529–30 | Mass glassing; fighting retreat |
| VI · Relic | 2530–31 | *Spirit of Fire* arrives; the relic is seized |
| VII · Ashfall | 2531–34 | Final glassing; the archive burns |

**Fun over balance; canon sets the anchors, players shape everything between.** Players feel powerful at every tier, and enemies never become bullet sponges; the danger is the war, not the enemy in front of them. Difficulty comes from distance to support, enemy density and objective complexity, so a lost deep mission feels like "we spent the orbital strike too early," never "the enemy was too strong." Death is expected, and drop pods make it a spectacle. Players write the tactical ledger with no limits; the story writes the strategic one. Every player victory raises FLEETCOM's confidence, which sends more ships and industry to Harvest, which draws more Covenant forces, which makes the collapse bigger (Appendix E). The community lives through the same false confidence that doomed the real campaign.

**War states are the content engine.** Helldivers 2 already proves the model: its Game Master chooses the Major Orders and where enemies attack, while "the war fought and liberation of planets — that's all you" ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/helldivers-2-director-explains-the-limits-of-joel-the-game-masters-power)). Its major turns are set, and players meet them as they arrive. REDACTED works the same way. Canon fixes where Harvest's war ends; the orchestrators decide when and how each turn lands; players decide everything between: which cities stand, which supply lines hold, which prototypes survive and how much FLEETCOM commits. Those war states carry into the next era and change its maps, arsenals, enemies and missions, so no two lineages play alike. Arrowhead's CEO has said he would "build automation on the gamemaster" to keep Helldivers 2's war running indefinitely ([GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/third-person-shooter/helldivers-2-ceo-wants-the-galactic-war-to-last-forever-even-if-arrowhead-has-to-sunset-the-game-in-the-far-future-and-the-devs-have-to-make-an-ai-game-master/)). REDACTED starts there.

**Progression grows wide, not tall.** Veterans earn more options, not bigger numbers, so a new player can answer any call for help. Core weapons and vehicles unlock per era; Procurement Contracts from canon manufacturers work like Warbonds and never expire; ONI Black Budget prototypes unlock through field use and clearance and are never for sale. The catalogues tell the story too: contracts multiply through the buildup, and in Glassfall every one is stamped "Procurement terminated."

**Invasions are the Covenant's side of the war.** The only way to play the Covenant is as an invader: a commander who drops a small AI lance into a hard mission, limited per operation. The invaded squad can call a quick reaction force, and other players answer by Pelican or ODST drop pod. The UNSC is favored to win, and the rescue becomes the story players tell.

**Spartans are rare, and earned.** Canon's Spartan-II program is small, so REDACTED never fields Spartans in numbers. Players serve as Marines, ODSTs, vehicle crews and pilots. A Spartan-II fireteam is a call-in the war economy has to earn, one per Battlefront at most, and Red Team fights in Era VI as *Halo Wars* records. Every Spartan operation beyond canon's sits in the Redacted tier.

**Players build the war too.** Forge ships at launch with map, mission and scenario tools built on the studio's own toolset. Approved community missions are tagged by the problem they solve, and when the war hits that problem the orchestrators can deploy them. Creators are credited in the archive, and Wargames hosts anything-goes custom games, PvP included, outside canon.

**Grand Theaters are the option on top.** A Grand Theater joins hundreds of players in one region from the ground to low orbit, with frigates and Covenant cruisers overhead. The largest shipped Unreal Engine 5 shooters run about 100 players in a battle, so it is built after the core loop, tested at Gate 2 against its player count and server budget, and ships only if it passes. With it, REDACTED launches as the $60 Theater edition; without it, as the $50 Battlefront edition, and Grand Theaters can still arrive later as a paid theater expansion.

## Why it wins

**The category's hits share five traits. REDACTED has all five, and adds what neither leader launched with.** Helldivers 2 and ARC Raiders are both squad games with one tight core loop, a premium price and a world players recognize at a glance, and Helldivers 2 turned its live war into a story the whole community follows. REDACTED is built on the same pattern, then adds 64-player combined-arms Battlefronts on Halo's vehicle sandbox, player-commanded Covenant invasions, a globally known franchise and creator tools from launch, and it matches ARC Raiders' day-one release on all three platforms, which Helldivers 2 took 18 months to reach.

**The failures share the opposite pattern.** Concord was a $40 PvP hero shooter in a new, generic world; it reportedly cost more than $200M to make ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/10/30/concord-budget-200-million-without-firewalk-acquisition)) and shut down two weeks after launch ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/gallery/live-service-graveyard-all-the-games-shut-down-far-too-soon/2900-5747/)). Price did not separate the winners from the failures: Space Marine 2 sold at $60 while Avengers and Anthem failed at $60. The loop did. REDACTED puts squad PvE first, has no gear grind, and rests on Halo's combat and vehicle sandbox, which has held players for 25 years.

**It holds up when players leave and expands when they come back.** Most live-service games have one minimum population, and below it they close. REDACTED has five operating modes, each needing fewer players and costing less than the one above:

| Mode | What runs | Live team (Battlefront / Theater) | Server cost per active player | Spend and expansion revenue retained |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Full war | Every unlocked era live, every scale, extra shards when demand exceeds plan; the signal to fund further expansions | 160 / 180 | 100% | 100% |
| 2 · Consolidated | Adjacent eras merge into fewer fronts | 115 / 130 | 90% | 92% |
| 3 · Single front | One live war advances an era each season | 80 / 90 | 80% | 80% |
| 4 · Forge-led | Expansions ship assets and tools; the war runs as a seasonal event | 40 / 45 | 60% | 60% |
| 5 · Archive | Campaign, Forge and player-hosted custom servers stay playable indefinitely | 8 / 8 | 20% | 30%, no expansions |

AI troops fill every battle, so no mode depends on a full server. Modes change only at seasonal resets and run in both directions: an expansion that brings players back reopens fronts. Each season the war runs in the mode with the best live margin, so live cost falls with the population, and the game never has to be switched off. That also answers the Stop Killing Games movement, whose European Citizens' Initiative drew a formal reply from the European Commission in June 2026 ([European Commission](https://citizens-initiative.europa.eu/news/european-commission-replies-stop-destroying-videogames-initiative-2026-06-16_en)).

**The result is a game built on the winners' pattern, with Halo's sandbox, Halo's name and Activision's reach on top.**

## The franchise platform

**Harvest is the product; the platform is the upside.** Both editions launch Harvest with Eras I–IV, Battlefronts, Invasions, and Map and Mission Forge; the Theater edition adds one Grand Theater region. Eras V–VII arrive in the first live year and complete the war. From year two, a paid theater expansion each year opens new ground at $20.

&#91;embedded content: Platform path · Harvest's eras and expansions, the self-funding gate, new wars, and pipeline reuse for later Halo games\]

**The win condition is a live war that pays for its own growth.** In year t the live war runs in the mode m that earns the most over its own running cost:

`m_t = argmax_m [ ρ_m · Ñ_t · (s_t + a · p · [t ≥ 2] · [m ≤ 4]) · f  −  ( T_m + κ_m · k · A_t ) ]`

Here Ñ\_t is effective owners (buyers plus Game Pass players weighted at half an owner), s\_t = s · (1 − d)^(t−1) is gross in-game spend per owner in year t, a is the expansion attach rate, p is the $20 expansion price, f is the net share after platform fees and ρ\_m is the share of spend and attach a mode retains. On the cost side, T\_m is the mode's live team cost, k is server cost per active owner-year ($3.10 in the Battlefront edition, $4.10 in the Theater edition), κ\_m is the mode's server factor, and A\_t = Ñ\_t · (1 − d)^(t−1) is the active population. The live war is self-funding in any year the chosen mode's margin is positive.

On Base-case terms, the first live year runs a full war on a planned loss while Eras V–VII ship. From year two the Base case earns more running a Forge-led seasonal war than a full one, and it funds itself there:

| Base case, Battlefront edition | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Year 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owners, including Game Pass players | 4.4M | 6.9M | 7.7M | 8.3M | 8.7M |
| Mode | 1 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Live revenue ($M) | 29 | 30 | 28 | 26 | 24 |
| Live cost ($M) | 43 | 16 | 14 | 12 | 11 |
| Break-even owners in that mode | 6.5M | 3.3M | 3.6M | 3.7M | 3.8M |

The Theater edition follows the same path at a higher cost: $51M in year one, then $19M–$13M a year in Mode 4. A Battlefront breakout holds Mode 1 in every year; a Theater breakout consolidates to Mode 2 in years four and five. In the Downside and Failure cases the war steps to the archive after year one: live cost over five years falls to $36M–$48M in present value instead of the $128M–$160M a full war would burn, and the archive runs at a net cost of about $1M a year.

Above the Mode 1 line every expansion is profit. Mode 1 runs at full strength, with extra shards added as demand requires, and a full war is the signal to fund the next theater. The same economy, orchestrators, arsenals and Forge tools then carry to other wars in Halo's history: Reach before its fall, the Insurrection, the Halo rings and other settings. Each new war is a content project on a proven platform, not a new game.

**Built on Halo's Unreal Engine 5 pipeline.** Halo Studios moved Halo from its proprietary Slipspace engine to Unreal Engine 5 in 2024, through an internal research effort called Project Foundry ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo:_Campaign_Evolved)), and built Halo-specific tools and plugins to make Unreal work for the franchise ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/halo-campaign-evolved-unreal-engine-5-preview/)). Halo: Campaign Evolved shipped on that pipeline on PC, PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S on July 28, 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo:_Campaign_Evolved)). REDACTED builds on it for four reasons:

- **It is the only Halo pipeline that exists today.** Campaign Evolved rebuilt Halo's original arsenal and Covenant roster in Unreal Engine 5; Harvest's Warthogs, Scorpions, Ghosts, Banshees and Wraiths start from those builds rather than from nothing.
- **The category's evidence is on Unreal Engine 5.** ARC Raiders, the category's newest breakout, is built on it ([Unreal Engine](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/embark-studios-build-the-award-winning-arc-raiders-with-unreal-engine)), and Squad runs 100-player combined-arms battles with vehicles on Unreal Engine 5.5 ([Squad](https://www.joinsquad.com/updates/squad-9-0-release-notes)). Activision has not named an engine for its Halo game ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/activision-halo-game-everything-know-140439377.html)); this proposal recommends Unreal Engine 5 for the reasons above. The credit is booked in full only on a franchise engine decision, which Foundation makes.

**REDACTED is built to be that game.** Activision has given its new Halo team one goal: "make the greatest Halo game ever" ([GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/activision-prepares-to-make-the-greatest-halo-game-ever-as-it-takes-control-of-the-franchise-from-halo-studios/)). **It is the purpose-built team's game.** Activision has already begun assembling a purpose-built Halo team, separate from Call of Duty ([GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/activision-prepares-to-make-the-greatest-halo-game-ever-as-it-takes-control-of-the-franchise-from-halo-studios/)). **The live war returns answers to the franchise.** From launch, REDACTED shows Microsoft, with its own player data:

Every Halo game after it, whatever its story, launches on proven tools, a tested pipeline and an audience that has been playing Halo for years.

The first $7M funds months 0–6 on approval.

**Preproduction answers eight questions,** each feeding a gate:

1. **Outcomes:** the full comparable-launch record, extended beyond the 14 titles in the appendix, and the traits that separated hits from failures. Feeds Gate 3.
2. **Demand by platform and price:** who buys, on which store, at $50 and at $60, and how strongly Master Chief fatigue shapes interest in a Chief-free Halo. Feeds Gates 2 and 3.
3. **Spend per owner:** willingness to pay for Procurement Contracts and theater expansions, and Game Pass uptake. Feeds Gate 3.
4. **War-state variance:** how far each era's outcome changes the maps, arsenals, enemies and missions of the next, across thousands of agent-simulated lineages and the playtest lineages. Feeds Gate 2.
5. **Population:** which operating mode the game should launch in. Feeds Gate 3.
6. **Budget and staffing:** the bottom-up production budget and studio plan for the chosen edition, including the Foundation group that serves later Halo titles. Feeds Gate 3.
7. **Grand Theater cost:** server cost per player-hour at full scale. Feeds Gate 2.
8. **Portfolio overlap:** how much of REDACTED's revenue is new to Activision. Feeds Gate 3.

**The team scales with the gates.** Preproduction opens with a REDACTED lead team of about 40 drawn from Activision's purpose-built Halo team, and grows to about 140 by month 18. Production follows Call of Duty's lead-and-support model: the lead team owns the sandbox, the vehicle roster, the orchestrators and, in the Theater edition, Grand Theater technology; Foundation owns the Unreal Engine 5 pipeline and asset library; and support studios each own era theaters. Production averages 220 people in the Battlefront edition and 260 in the Theater edition, peaking at about 330 in the Theater edition. Halo Studios reviews canon throughout, World's Edge advises on the war economy, and Rare advises on the shared world and invasions.

**Agents are in the pipeline from month one.** The orchestrators are AI agents, so the same simulation plays thousands of wars overnight to tune the economy, difficulty bands and mission mix before a human playtest. Human playtests then focus on what agents cannot judge: feel, fun and story.

## Appendix

### A. Budget model

All budget and return figures come from the REDACTED budget model (`redacted_model.py`). Costs are built from headcount by phase:

- **Labor:** a blended $199.5K per person-year, from $240K at the lead studio, $180K at support studios and $90K for outsourcing, split 55/30/15. The lead-studio rate is the mean wage for software developers in the Seattle area, Halo's home market ($164,130 in May 2023, [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes151252.htm)), grossed up to total employer cost at the private-industry wage share of about 70% ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm)), which gives about $234K, rounded to $240K for wage growth since.
- **Non-labor costs:** 15% of labor, for tools, middleware, motion capture, hardware and licensing.
- **Contingency:** 15%, about the sum of Gate 3's 10% budget band and the 5.8% agent-assisted saving that Gate 2 has yet to confirm.
- **Agent-assisted saving:** the conservative scenario in Appendix B, 5.8% of development.
- **Editions:** Theater production averages 260 people for $161M; Battlefront drops the Grand Theater technology and region team, about 40 people, for $136M.
- **Marketing:** 60% of development, below Raph Koster's AAA benchmark of 75–100% ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-cost-of-games)) because Activision and Xbox own major channels. Spent 15% in months 24–36, 35% in months 36–48 and 50% in the launch year.
- **Phases:** 18 months of preproduction (tranches of $7M, $11M and $16M over months 0–6, 6–12 and 12–18), 30 months of production spent evenly over months 18–48, and live operations from month 48.
- **Live operations:** a team cost of $183K per live head-year at the mode's headcount, plus servers at about $4.10 per active owner-year in the Theater edition and $3.10 in the Battlefront edition, scaled by the mode's server factor. The Grand Theater cost study replaces the server rate with cost per player-hour.
- **Franchise reuse credit:** 15% of development, carried into later Halo titles; the engine share depends on whether they stay on Unreal Engine 5.

**Revenue** is valued over five years at launch:

`PV_rev = Σ (t = 1…5) [ Σ_q c_(t,q) · P · (1 − φ_q) + ρ_(m_t) · ( Ñ_t · s · (1 − d)^(t−1) · f + Ñ_t · a · p · f · [t ≥ 2] · [m_t ≤ 4] ) ] ÷ (1 + r)^(t−1)`

Here c\_(t,q) is copies sold in year t on platform q (55%, 20%, 12%, 8% and 5% of lifetime copies, split 50/25/25 across Steam, PlayStation and Xbox, with Xbox copies from year two halved by Game Pass), P is the realized price (87.5% of the $50 or $60 list), φ\_q is the platform fee (Valve's tiered share on the game's lifetime Steam revenue, 30% on PlayStation, and 0% or 30% on Xbox by view), f = 1 − Σ\_q w\_q φ\_q is the blended net share, Ñ\_t = O\_t + μ · G\_t is effective owners (cumulative buyers O\_t plus Game Pass players G\_t weighted by μ = 0.5), s is gross in-game spend per owner in year one, d is the 30% annual decline in spend and engagement, a is the expansion attach rate, p is the $20 expansion price, m\_t is the operating mode, ρ\_m is the share of spend and attach the mode retains and r is the 10% discount rate.

**Program cost** is dated to the same point:

`C = Σ_j K_j · (1 + r)^((48 − τ_j) / 12) + Σ (t = 1…5) [ T_(m_t) + κ_(m_t) · k · A_t ] ÷ (1 + r)^(t−1) − R_reuse`

where K\_j is each month's development and marketing spend at month τ\_j from approval, T\_m is the mode's live team cost, κ\_m its server factor, k the edition's server rate, A\_t = Ñ\_t · (1 − d)^(t−1) the active population and R\_reuse the reuse credit.

**Mode ladder:** m\_1 = 1 while Eras V–VII ship; for t ≥ 2 the war runs in the mode with the best live margin:

`m_t = argmax_m [ L_t(m) − T_m − κ_m · k · A_t ]`

with L\_t(m) the in-game and expansion revenue of year t in mode m.

**Returns:** `NPV = PV_rev − C` and `multiple = PV_rev ÷ C`. Copies needed for a multiple M solve `PV_rev(N) = M · C(N)`; both sides depend on N, because live and server costs scale with the population.

### E. The confidence trap

FLEETCOM's commitment grows with the victories it wins, and victories grow with what FLEETCOM commits. Let A\_k be the UNSC force committed in era k, C\_k the Covenant pressure and E\_k the community's play in that era. Success depends on both forces:

`I_k = γ · A_k − δ · C_k + E_k`

and each side escalates in response:

`A_(k+1) = A_k + α · I_k,    C_(k+1) = C_k + β · A_k,    α, β, γ, δ > 0`

In state form x\_(k+1) = M · x\_k + b\_k with x = (A, C):

`M = [ 1 + αγ   −αδ ]
    [   β       1  ]`

Its determinant is 1 + αγ + αβδ > 1, so the escalation grows from era to era. The discriminant of its characteristic polynomial, α²γ² − 4αβδ, sets how it grows:

- **αγ² > 4βδ:** real eigenvalues, the largest λ = 1 + αγ/2 + ½√(α²γ² − 4αβδ) > 1. Commitment and pressure climb together in a steady surge.
- **αγ² < 4βδ:** complex eigenvalues of modulus √(1 + αγ + αβδ) > 1. Offensives and counteroffensives swing back and forth with growing amplitude.

Either way, community play E\_k feeds I\_k, which raises A, which raises C. The loss at Glassfall, the force committed when the authored collapse lands, rises with every era of good play:

`L = A_V,    ∂L / ∂E_k > 0 for every k < V`

The better players do, the larger the loss they live through. α, β, γ and δ are the orchestrators' tuning parameters, and the sign of αγ² − 4βδ chooses between a surge and a see-saw war for each lineage.
